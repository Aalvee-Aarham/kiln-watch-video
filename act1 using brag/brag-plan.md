# Brag Plan: Kiln Watch · Act 1 (WHO), 0:00–0:46

## What is this app?
Kiln Watch harmonizes NASA's MODIS and VIIRS fire records into one comparable 23-year record, so anyone can tell whether a fire season is truly unusual. It's Team Off The Binary's NASA Space Apps 2026 entry for "Harmonization of MODIS and VIIRS Hot Spots".

## The angle
The world's fire record broke in 2012. The video shows it breaking with NASA's own detections, then makes it human: a real Bangladesh forest that burned twice in four days in March 2025, and a team that breathes this smoke every winter. Every visual is real data, real imagery or real footage from Bangladesh. The intro ends unresolved, opening into the world map Act 2 begins on.

## Hook (first 2–3 seconds)
A calm, photographic Earth (NASA Blue Marble, softly graded) with real fire detections blooming season by season while a year counter ticks from 2003. It's quiet and beautiful, then 2012 hits.

## Key moments (the middle)
- **2012:** every continent fills with detections at once, the raw line shoots up, and "2012: FIRES EXPLODE?" slams in.
- **The cause, on real pixels:** Rangamati, 12 Apr 2013. One 1.1 km MODIS pixel against nine 375 m VIIRS pixels over the same fires, with the counter reading 1 vs 9.
- **Bhaluka:** one continuous zoom from globe to Bangladesh to Bhaluka at dusk, then a real Mymensingh-division sal forest, the Prothom Alo report (25 Mar 2025) and the two fires as date cards.
- **Team:** six cards deal in one by one over real brick-kiln smoke in Gazipur, next to Dhaka's winter PM2.5 curve.

## Outro / punchline
"Is this season worse than normal?" types itself over the continents, the raw chart says YES, and UNRELIABLE stamps it. The globe then unwraps into the flat world fire map that Act 2's quiz opens on. There is no logo and no resolution.

## User flow worth showing
Kiln Watch in use, from the live site: the Explorer map, then a tap on the 2012 jump in the Sensors chart (0:37 montage).
